What I do

Sales governance and method

Three to six months

Most companies do not lack sales activity; they lack a way to tell which of it matters. Deals get qualified on confidence, forecasts on hope, and governance is either absent or so heavy the team works around it.

I assess how the company actually sells, then design and implement a sales governance framework fit for purpose: qualification standards, deal stages with evidence-based exits, an operating cadence and CRM discipline. It is sized to the sales motions in place: light where deals are standard, thorough only where they are not.

The playbook, the CRM set-up and the reports are yours, and within ninety days the cadence runs by your own people.

Sales organisation design

Three to six months

The portfolio and the sales motion have drifted apart: the team is selling the way it sold five years ago, into a market that has moved. Growth stalls and nobody can say precisely where.

I assess the portfolio against market segment and sales motion, match it against what the team can actually do, and realign the organisation around where it can win.

Assessment, target operating model, role definitions (including the split between winning new clients and growing existing ones), KPIs, and the transition run with you rather than handed over as a report.

Commercial due diligence

Four to eight weeks

The growth plan says one thing. The sales engine underneath it may say another. Before an acquisition or an investment, someone has to test whether the commercial machine can deliver the number in the model.

An operator's challenge of the plan: sales-engine maturity, pipeline quality, portfolio maturity, organisation and leadership, delivery against ambition, and what a stronger owner could unlock.

For corporate development, transaction services teams, investors in IT services and software, and groups that acquire firms one after another.

Market entry and go-to-market

Three to six months

A firm has an offer that works at home and a market it has never sold into. A wrong first move costs a year and the references the next attempt depends on.

I assess the candidate markets, segment the buyers by how they actually buy, and test the findings with primary research at executive level. Then I design the route in: direct, partner-led or phased.

Market assessment, buyer segmentation, partner model (if applicable), and a costed phased entry plan with a revenue model.

Major-deal steering

Six to twelve months, or per pursuit

Big deals are lost long before the bid. They are lost by qualifying in late, by shaping nothing, by discovering the price-to-win in the final week, and by governance that reviews slides instead of decisions.

I install a steering framework that starts before the RFP: qualification gates, a scorecard that bites, decision-unit mapping, price-to-win, win strategy and a close plan, and I chair it until your own people can.

Gate structure, scorecard, pursuit templates, two-layer governance, and the team trained to run it without me.
