Solution Sales Isn’t Dead. It’s Just Not Enough.

Seller skill no longer wins major deals on its own.

Jan Cleeren · November 2026 · 6 pages

Get the full paper

Executive summary

In IT services, seller skill is no longer enough to win major deals. The binding constraint is whether management can see and steer the client’s decision while it is still being formed. Most firms cannot.

Fifty years of method have each added a question the seller must be able to answer, from “what does the product do?” to “how will this client decide, and who must agree?” The skills are well documented and widely trained. Yet buyers now do most of the work without suppliers, increasingly with AI, and often with a hyperscaler or sourcing adviser shaping the decision first. Deals are lost less to competitors than to indecision, and AI is changing the size and the pricing of the deals themselves.

None of that is fixed by training alone. It is fixed by how leaders steer their major deals: choosing early which deals to shape, reviewing them at a few decisive moments, and judging progress by what the client has done rather than what the seller has said.

Fifty years in five questions

Each era of selling added a question without retiring the previous one. What changed each time was where the decisive conversation took place.

Six questions for sales leaders

Answer them for your ten largest open deals and your five largest renewals.

Did we know about this deal before the client wrote its requirements?Most major deals were known and influenced earlyYou first heard of it when the RFP arrived

More than two warning signs suggest the constraint is not seller skill alone. It is how the organisation decides where and when to compete.
